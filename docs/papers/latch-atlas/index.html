Abstract

What it is. A cross-domain atlas of topological latches: whole numbers held by a closed loop that only a cut can change. It puts the same four questions to thirteen media and reads each with one instrument, the loop walk (holonomy): add up the turning round a closed loop and the total is an integer. The media are the superconducting ring (flux quantization, the rf SQUID), the ring-shaped superfluid (persistent current, quantized circulation), closed circular DNA (linking number, twist and writhe, supercoiling, topoisomerases and gyrase), magnetic skyrmions (skyrmion number as solid angle), nematic liquid crystals (disclination strength in halves), vortex rings and linked vortex lines in water (helicity), plasma and the Sun (magnetic helicity as linking weighted by flux, Taylor relaxation, reconnection), the electric power grid (winding number of the voltage phase round a loop of lines, loop flows), light, adio and sound with orbital angular momentum (twisted beams, topological charge), a lattice ring model and a bare filament. Each medium gets a one-page card that prices its count: what is stored on each rung, how high the wall between rungs stands in units of thermal energy (kT), how the count leaks, and what writes it.

What it finds. Matter latches and waves label: a twisted beam carries a whole number that holds nothing, until it lands on matter whose phase must close on itself. A count is whole only when it is read as a sum of closed pieces (the integer diagnostic: an exact solid-angle read returns −1.000000000000 where the smooth formula on the same grid does not). A superconducting ring with one weak spot is a gearbox that writes one rung at a time and erases by a second write from the other side, paying the same price twice (hysteresis as memory). What a write costs is the wall, not the store. Temperature is the write-enable: freezing a ring through its transition writes a whole number by thermal odds or by Kibble–Zurek domains, and the two routes give one staircase that no field scan can tell apart. A ring of N emitters writes a twist only below N/2, the read side’s half-turn limit met from the write side. A lock stalls at a quarter turn, and a powered lock (Josephson voltage standard, Kuramoto synchronization, the grid) is not a latch. Read from real SDO/HMI SHARP vector magnetograms through two X-class flares (NOAA 11166 and 11158), loops whose rims stayed strong kept their whole numbers, 1,589 and 1,352 of them, not one changing; the flares did their work where no rim held, and the instrument’s 180-degree ambiguity turned out to be a pen in the reader’s own hand.

What it is made of. Every number comes from a named run of one of 64 small tools (pure mathematics or stated models unless a section says otherwise) or from a named source, and every section ends with two lines saying which works it read and how far, and which runs it rests on. The reference list gives the depth of reading for every one of its 201 entries, and a “Who came before” list credits the people who met the same walls first. The tools, their run ledgers and figures, and a manifest with the hash of every file travel with the book. Written with Claude (Anthropic), as the Declaration states.

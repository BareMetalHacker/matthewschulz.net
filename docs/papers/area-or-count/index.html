Abstract

A trick roper at the county fair can show, with one rope, the four places a loop keeps what it leaves behind. A flat loop spun beside you sweeps more floor the bigger it gets: area. The wedding ring spun round you goes round you once, big or small: count. A breakaway string snaps only when the run takes the rope to its end, and stays snapped: reach. Jump through the spinning loop and back, the Texas skip, and you have two leapfrogging rings, whose leftover lives in the slope of their loop's area.

This paper asks which of these a loop reads, with four toy models, each registered with a lean and a rival before its code existed: a paper cone with its tip rounded off, holes that each flip a loop's sign, a shaken sled on a rail whose dead edge gives it a memory (the rotor bench of Past the Dead Edge), and two thin vortex rings leapfrogging in an ideal fluid.

What it found. A count dies under disorder at a rate set by area: with a share f of grid holes moved, the loop's mean sign falls as exp(−k·f), k = 4πr²(1 − q), within 10 per cent in all five judged cells. The sled's switch reads neither area nor count: five loops of equal area do not write alike, repeated laps leave the same stored height to four decimals, and a held sled is let go only when the loop turns home past its far corner's mirror across the seam at dial 90, where two far corners and 18 turn points each flip exactly once. The sled's resting places follow the drift law of a sled shaken without a coil, read at the coil's local strength: 113 of 115 within 0.02, registered first. With the coil's grip removed the same knob loops write an area, each big loop equal to the sum of its six cells to 0.001 per cent, and the same area can be read from single strokes of the knobs, each leg driven alone both ways, to 0.0083 per cent, without driving any loop. The rings' advance per cycle is minus the slope of their loop's area against push, at fixed energy, and the cycle's time is the slope against energy, both to 2 parts in 10,000 or better; near the point where the rings would sit in one place the loop sweeps area at the rings' strength alone, to 0.022 per cent, so area and count become one reading. On the cone, twenty noisy circles recover the width of the rounded cap within 10 per cent in all 500 trials.

The rule these support: a loop leaves an area when the machine has a free coordinate and one resting state per setting of its knobs, a reach when it has more than one resting state, and nothing when it is pinned with one. Of the paper's registered predictions, 14 landed and 14 missed; 9 of the misses were redesigned into a prediction that landed, and every miss is listed with what it taught. Known results are named beside the ones they meet: the splitting of the rings' travel into a dynamic and a geometric part (Shashikanth and Marsden, 2003), the wiping-out memory of Preisach-type hysteresis, and Stokes's theorem and the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff series behind reading an area from single moves.

The upload holds the paper (PDF and its markdown source) and one zip, AREA_OR_COUNT_2026-11-14_code_and_data.zip, of every script, printout, data file and figure behind its numbers, 99 files; AOC_PROVENANCE inside it lists each run's files and prints every file's md5. The rotor bench's core and two of its runs, from Past the Dead Edge, and the Loop Atlas's ring and cone tools ship unchanged from those records.

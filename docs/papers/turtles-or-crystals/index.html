Abstract

A speculative, openly-licensed synthesis paper (preprint, not peer-reviewed) asking whether life's substrate must be carbon chemistry or could rest on crystal instead — "turtles all the way down," asked of life. Drawing on artificial life, the mineral origin-of-life tradition, information-theoretic accounts of life, condensed-matter defect physics, and quasispecies theory, it proposes that a single topologically protected lattice defect could serve three functions at once — heritable memory, the shaping of form, and the transduction of mechanical state into electrical signal — and that such a substrate would behave as a self-healing, fault-tolerant information network. Two falsifiable bridges are advanced as hypotheses: that topological protection is the physical fix for the low-fidelity wall that ended the mineral-heredity program, and that a crystalline replicator possesses an Eigen error threshold set by its defect-copying fidelity. The individual ingredients are established; the contribution is their synthesis plus concrete testable predictions on existing platforms. Every seam between grounded physics and conjecture is marked.

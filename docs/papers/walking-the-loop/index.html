Abstract

A short, equation-free primer for the shared intuition behind a portable loop-holonomy instrument that recurs across fields — astronomy, geodesy, crystallography, molecular topology — which otherwise do not share a vocabulary. In plain terms it builds three ideas from one everyday example: that carrying a pointer around a closed loop can bring it home turned, and that the turning comes from the curvature the loop encloses, never from the loop's shape (a flat loop comes home unturned; the same loop on a globe comes home turned); that a loop also carries a whole-number "winding" count that noise cannot fake; and that a "loop-walk" reads both the turn (holonomy) and the count (winding) from any data with a direction at each place. The earthquake fault-junction — plates that rub, push, and slide, where we cannot predict quakes but can measure the strain as a rotation — is used as the intuitive anchor. Written as a standalone companion to the author's loop-holonomy papers across substrates. Two figures are placed (flat-versus-curved holonomy; winding 0/1/2); remaining illustration slots are marked.
